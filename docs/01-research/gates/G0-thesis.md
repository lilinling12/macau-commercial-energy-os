# G0 Thesis & Market Rationale

**Status:** SUBSTANTIALLY COMPLETE for the original research rationale according to the current roadmap; target-customer/problem and commercial validation remain OPEN.
**Purpose:** Establish whether Macau commercial sites have a specific, frequent and consequential energy decision that an Energy OS can improve, identify who experiences/owns/pays for it, and determine whether a feasible first product and pilot are justified.
**Authority:** Original G0–G7 research framework; current `docs/00-authority/ROADMAP.md`; research-derived `docs/02-product/PRODUCT-DESIGN.md` and PRD v0.1; customer discovery and usability protocol v0.1.
**Boundary:** Aggregate market statistics, technology trends, a polished prototype or favorable concept reaction do not validate customer demand, buying authority, willingness to pay, or a measurable customer outcome.

## Research questions

1. Which Macau commercial-site segment has a recurring, high-impact energy/operations problem within the proposed scope?
2. Who experiences the work, who owns the workflow, who recommends/approves changes, who controls data access, and who pays?
3. What recent bills, demand events, plant constraints, data-quality failures or settlement disputes trigger work today, and what consequences follow?
4. Which existing internal process, BMS/EMS, consultant, energy-service contract or spreadsheet already addresses the need, and where does it fail?
5. What evidence do operations, finance and asset owners require to trust an economic result or recommendation?
6. What meter/BMS/asset/contract data exists, who owns it, what access is permitted, and what integration/security burden would a pilot create?
7. Which product workflow and outcome would be valuable enough to justify a pilot, procurement effort and recurring use?
8. What evidence supports proceeding, narrowing the segment, changing the product promise or stopping?

## Evidence rules

- Separate official/public statistics and policies, customer/site artifacts, observed workflow, participant recollection, commercial commitments and project assumptions.
- Do not treat sector energy totals or a reported practice as proof of an individual building's pain, flexibility, savings opportunity or purchase intent.
- Prefer a recent concrete event and, where authorized, redacted bills, logs, meter/BMS reports, procurement records or workflow artifacts over hypothetical opinion.
- Distinguish end user, workflow owner, approver, data owner, buyer and budget owner; job title alone does not establish decision authority.
- A favorable prototype reaction is usability/concept feedback, not evidence of purchase intent. Hypothetical pricing response alone is not willingness to pay.
- Record contradictory evidence, sample limitations, recruitment path and whether the participant/site permitted the proposed follow-up.
- The discovery protocol's proposed 8–12 initial interviews across roles and at least two contexts is a qualitative sampling plan, not a statistical market estimate or automatic Gate threshold.

## Required outputs

1. A versioned problem/thesis statement with named target segment hypotheses, user/buyer roles, jobs and explicit non-goals.
2. Evidence-linked examples of recent user work, frequency, impact, existing alternatives and failure points.
3. A role and decision map distinguishing user, workflow owner, approver, data owner and buyer/budget owner.
4. A data-access and pilot-feasibility inventory: candidate site records, owners, permission path, integration constraints and known costs/risks.
5. Product-value hypotheses with measurable outcome candidates and the evidence each would require; distinguish modeled benefit from customer-measured result.
6. A discovery/usability evidence log with recruitment context, artifact consent, contradictions, synthesis and unresolved questions.
7. A reviewed continue/narrow/pivot/stop decision, with named approver, date, supporting evidence and residual assumptions.

## Exit criteria

G0 may close only when the evidence packet supports a reviewed decision that:

- identifies a specific Macau commercial customer/site segment and consequential job, with direct evidence from relevant people and/or authorized artifacts;
- distinguishes the user, workflow owner, buyer/budget holder, approver and data-access owner, or records why any role remains uncertain;
- documents current alternatives and the demonstrated gap that the product is intended to address;
- establishes a plausible, permissioned data path and pilot discovery path, with material integration/procurement constraints visible;
- records validated versus unvalidated product outcomes and any commercial evidence without overstating stated interest as a commitment;
- resolves the initial product thesis to proceed, narrow, pivot or stop, and updates PRD/product scope and open questions accordingly.

The current roadmap's “substantially complete” status refers to the earlier research rationale; it does not mean customer discovery, willingness-to-pay validation, product approval or pilot acceptance is complete.
